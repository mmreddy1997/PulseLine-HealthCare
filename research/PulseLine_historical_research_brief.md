# PulseLine: historical evidence behind financial pressure

Prepared September 12, 2026 from the user's research narrative and attached five-hospital analysis. This document records research direction and supplied claims; it does not independently verify the cited financial figures or event dates. It supplements the three-hospital evidence pack without changing its observations or event labels.

## Product purpose

“Understand financial pressure at rural Kentucky hospitals.”

County and state healthcare leaders should be able to inspect the evidence behind an indicator: what changed, the reporting entity, the period measured, when the information became public, and its source. Low-income rural residents are intended beneficiaries. PulseLine is exploratory decision support, not a validated bankruptcy, closure, service-loss or acquisition predictor.

## Research question

Were pre-event changes at Paul B. Hall and Highlands different from changes at hospitals experiencing financial pressure without the same identified acquisition event?

The supplied narrative suggests financial pressure may be shared across event and comparison hospitals. This is a hypothesis to test with consistent data, not an established finding of unusual trajectories, causation or predictive specificity. A two-case comparison can illustrate evidence handling; it cannot estimate reliable population-level specificity.

## Evidence domains to test

| Domain | Candidate inputs | Meaning and boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Financial | Net patient revenue, compatible operating expenses, cash, current assets/liabilities, total assets/liabilities | Trends within consistent reporting scope; IRS net income is not automatically HCRIS operating margin |
| Utilization | Discharges, inpatient days, bed-days available, census | Distinguish licensed, staffed and available beds; separate acute/swing-bed activity |
| Structural | Legal bankruptcy filing, restructuring, announced transaction, completed CHOW, property sale | Distinct events at distinct organizational levels; acquisition need not indicate distress |
| Workforce/access | Historical HPSA designation and aggregate workforce supply | County access context, not clinician departures; HPSA is a designation, not a migration count |
| Community | Population trajectory, poverty, age structure, payer context | Preserve geography, reference years and uncertainty; county population is not affected population |

“Signal convergence” is an experimental hypothesis. Correlated indicators are not independent evidence domains simply because the interface groups them separately. Define material change before inspecting event labels. Missing domains remain unknown, not normal or zero. Do not activate a score or claim “four domains deteriorated” until measurements and thresholds are documented.

## Interface evidence contract for engineering

For each item show: hospital/entity, observation, units, reporting period, public availability date, source link, source scope, verification state and limitation. Separate observed values, calculations, contextual events and later outcomes.

Suggested wording before verification: “This case explores which documented indicators were publicly available before a later event.”

After verification, “Multiple observable indicators were present before the later documented event” is appropriate only if those indicators and availability dates support it. Never say “PulseLine predicted the acquisition.” Publicly announced merger activity is a known transaction-development signal, not a hidden financial forecast.

## Completion gate

The first usable case requires a verified identity timeline; dated event evidence; identical financial/utilization fields for both hospitals; point-in-time availability evidence; compatible scope notes; and an explicit missing-data record. Compare a financial-only baseline with any added domains using the same observation set. Full model validation requires a substantially larger cohort and held-out time periods.
