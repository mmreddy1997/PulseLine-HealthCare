# PulseLine research category framework

Prepared September 12, 2026 from the project owner's proposed research framework. This is a planning taxonomy, not a verified account of every M&A team's practices, a completed dataset, or a validated prediction model.

## Areas and proposed indicators

| Area | M&A-oriented research questions | Candidate PulseLine indicators |
|---|---|---|
| Financial | Revenue, expenses, margins, cash, debt, assets and liabilities | Operating-margin trend, liquidity trend, expense versus revenue growth |
| Patient volume | Admissions, discharges, inpatient days and outpatient activity | Volume and occupancy trends |
| Payer mix | Medicare, Medicaid, commercial and uninsured activity | Government-payer concentration and payer-mix shifts |
| Workforce | Physician/nurse supply, recruitment and labor costs | Aggregate county provider supply, shortage designations and labor-cost trends |
| Service lines | Emergency, surgery, maternity, behavioral health and imaging availability | Dated service availability and verified reductions |
| Quality | Outcomes, safety, readmissions and patient experience | CMS quality measures with reporting periods, eligibility and suppression notes |
| Market | Population, competing facilities, referrals and market share | Population trajectory, nearby hospitals and rural isolation |
| Capital | Buildings, equipment, deferred maintenance and IT needs | Documented investment needs and capital-expenditure trends |
| Legal/regulatory | Licenses, compliance, investigations and contracts | Verified bankruptcy filings, CMS status changes and regulatory events |
| Ownership/structure | Parent, leases, subsidiaries and property ownership | Provider CHOW, restructuring and property transactions, separately classified |
| Strategic fit | Geography, referral network, specialties and expansion | Documented rationale for a hospital's importance to an acquiring system |

## Interpretation and collection rules

- These are candidate inputs. Do not imply the project has measured them merely because the category is listed.
- Preserve institution-level observations, county context, derived measures and analyst interpretations separately. No personal clinician histories or patient records.
- Use consistent financial scope. Gross charges are not collected revenue; total liabilities are not solely debt; IRS net income is not automatically hospital operating margin.
- Define payer mix by the actual denominator: revenue, discharges or patient days. Do not substitute one for another or infer commercial/uninsured shares from missing records.
- Define occupancy with compatible patient days and available bed-days. Licensed, staffed, acute and swing-bed counts are not interchangeable.
- HPSA and county workforce supply describe access context, not confirmed hospital employee departures.
- Missing quality measures and missing service listings are unknown, not poor quality or evidence of a service closure. Quality is a separate construct from financial distress.
- Low capital spending or depreciation alone does not establish deferred maintenance. Strategic fit requires documented evidence or an explicitly labeled analyst hypothesis.
- Property sale, provider acquisition, parent bankruptcy, operator bankruptcy, regulatory conversion and service reduction remain different events. If an event is relevant to two categories, store it once and reference it rather than counting it twice.
- None of these categories establishes insolvency, impending acquisition or service loss. Acquisition can occur for reasons unrelated to distress.

## Fit with the historical demonstration

Keep the first Paul B. Hall versus Morgan County comparison focused on consistent financial, utilization, structural, aggregate workforce/access and community observations. Payer mix can support the financial/context analysis; ownership and legal events form the structural timeline. Service lines, quality, capital and strategic fit remain optional extensions when comparable dated evidence is available.

Retain the same pre-event cutoff for both hospitals. Record the observation period, publication/availability date, source URL, access date, reporting scope, uncertainty and calculation inputs. Unknown public availability prevents a claim that an indicator was knowable before the event.
